# Teach stars as one causal loop

A star's whole life can be taught as a single loop in which gravity always pushes inward and only the source of support changes. Turbulence and magnetic fields hold up clouds, thermal pressure holds up protostars, fusion-maintained thermal pressure holds up main-sequence and giant stars, and degeneracy pressure holds up remnants. Each transition happens when a support runs out of fuel or softens to an adiabatic index of 4/3 or below. One causal chain links every stage: metallicity sets cooling, cooling sets the Jeans mass and infall rate, infall racing against feedback sets stellar mass, winds and companions turn initial mass into core mass, core mass and degeneracy decide the ending, and the ending's yields set the next generation's metallicity. For teaching, the evidence is just as clear about method. Unassisted discovery underperforms explicit instruction (d = −0.38 across 164 studies), while guided, feedback-rich discovery outperforms other methods (d = 0.50). Simulations beat no simulation (g = 0.62), and scaffolded simulations beat bare ones (g = 0.49). Interactive engagement roughly doubles conceptual gains in introductory astronomy. Novices arrive believing stars "burn" (44%) and naming size rather than mass as what distinguishes stars (only about 5.5% mention mass). An idle game is therefore well placed: it is inherently multi-session, its prestige reset can serve as a debrief, and it can make the real physics the progression mechanic. Its main risk is that exponential economies and "buy fusion" upgrades teach false causality unless game rules are visibly separated from physical laws. Part I below is a standalone physics reference and Part II is standalone teaching guidance. A closing map joins the two.

**How to read the evidence labels.** Physics claims use the vocabulary of Accretion's `FIDELITY.md`. **Established** means reviews and textbooks agree on the mechanism. **Simplified** means a published anchor exists but the game form reduces it. **Debated** means reviews call it open or models disagree. **Textbook (unchecked)** marks standard derivations, such as the virial theorem and Jeans scaling, that the researchers did not check against a fetched textbook this session. Teaching claims use **Strong** for meta-analyses, replicated multi-site studies or validated instruments, **Moderate** for one large or controlled study, **Weak** for case studies, think-alouds, small samples or practitioner craft, and **Design inference** for proposals derived from the evidence but never tested in a game. Several classic education papers (Hake, Crouch and Mazur, Kalyuga, Habgood and Ainsworth, Clark 2016) were cited from established literature without re-reading this session, so their exact figures need checking before they are quoted in-game.

# Part I — Physics reference: the stellar life cycle as a chain of mechanisms

## Gravity never stops; only the support against it changes

Stellar evolution is a sequence of quasi-equilibria, and the most useful single teaching idea is the support source at each stage. Giant molecular clouds are supersonically turbulent and weakly ionized. They have velocity dispersions of **1 to 10 km/s**, fields of **1 to 10 μG** and temperatures near **10 K**, so thermal pressure alone is far too weak to hold them up ([Krumholz 2014](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1402.0867v2.pdf)). Turbulence plays a dual role: it opposes gravity in some places and creates "overdensities to initiate gravitational contraction or collapse" in others. Turbulence, fields and self-gravity interact in a "highly nonlinear and multidimensional" way ([McKee & Ostriker 2007](https://www.astro.princeton.edu/~burrows/classes/514/papers/mckee.ostriker.0707.3514.pdf)). Protostars are supported thermally and heat as they contract. Accreting massive protostars pass through an explicit Kelvin-Helmholtz contraction phase before hydrogen ignites ([Hosokawa et al. 2011](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1111.3649)). On the main sequence, mass sets how hot and dense the core must be to hold up the overlying layers ([OpenStax 22.1](https://openstax.org/books/astronomy/pages/22-1-evolution-from-the-main-sequence-to-red-giants)). In the most massive stars radiation pressure takes a growing share of the support. The ZAMS mass-luminosity slope falls from about **3 at 10 to 20 M☉ to about 1.3 at 200 to 500 M☉** ([Yusof et al. 2013](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1305.2099)), and even a 300 M☉ star stays below the electron-scattering Eddington limit at Γ_e ≈ 0.57.

The endings follow the same logic. A massive star's core collapses because "the average adiabatic γ in the core is at or below 4/3," driven by photodissociation and relativistic electrons. The iron core has an effective Chandrasekhar mass of **1.25 to 2.0 M☉**, depending on the progenitor. Collapse stops when the nuclear equation of state stiffens at nuclear density, and the inner 0.5 to 0.6 M☉ rebounds "as a unit" ([Burrows 2013](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1210.4921)). Most low- and intermediate-mass stars instead end as carbon-oxygen white dwarfs supported by electron degeneracy. Super-AGB stars leave oxygen-neon white dwarfs, with a C-O core limit near 1.1 M☉ ([Karakas & Lattanzio 2014](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1405.0062)).

| Stage | What holds it up against gravity | What ends the stage | Label |
|---|---|---|---|
| Molecular cloud | Turbulence and magnetic fields (thermal pressure too weak) | Turbulent overdensities exceed the Jeans mass and cool | Established |
| Prestellar core and protostar | Thermal pressure; contraction heats it (Kelvin-Helmholtz) | Core reaches hydrogen-fusion temperature | Established |
| Main sequence | Thermal pressure maintained by H fusion (pp below about 1.2 M☉, CNO above) | Core hydrogen exhausted | Established |
| Very massive star | Large share from radiation pressure | Same, but lifetime flattens to about 2 to 3 Myr | Established |
| Giant branches | Shell burning; core contracts and heats | Next core ignition (He, then C, Ne, O, Si) or envelope loss | Established |
| White dwarf | Electron degeneracy | Stable unless a companion adds mass | Established |
| Neutron star | Neutron degeneracy plus nuclear forces | Stable unless pushed past its maximum mass (not researched here) | Established |
| Black hole | Nothing wins | — | Established |
| Pair-instability core | Pressure softens as heat becomes e⁺e⁻ rest mass | Pulses, total disruption, or direct collapse | Simplified (mechanism textbook, unchecked) |

Two textbook ideas carry the causal weight, and both are labelled Textbook (unchecked). First, the virial theorem gives a self-gravitating gas ball negative heat capacity: when it loses energy it contracts and gets hotter. That is why an exhausted core heats toward its next fuel instead of cooling off. Second, degeneracy pressure depends on density and not on temperature. A degenerate core that is heated does not expand and cool, so ignition in degenerate matter runs away, as in the helium flash and carbon ignition in a white dwarf. As electrons become relativistic the effective γ tends toward 4/3, which connects the white-dwarf mass limit to the core-collapse criterion above.

## Star formation is a race that feedback rigs toward 1% efficiency

Star formation is a contest between gravity and everything that heats or supports gas. Its headline outcome is inefficiency. Molecular-gas depletion times are about **100 times** cloud free-fall times, and the efficiency per free-fall time is about **ε_ff ≈ 0.01** across clouds and galaxies. That this number is low is Established. Why it is low is Debated ([Krumholz 2014](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1402.0867v2.pdf)). At cloud scale ε_ff is about 0.01 with 0.5 dex scatter, and whole-cloud efficiency is **4 to 10%** ([Utomo 2018](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.11121), [Chevance 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.03479)). McKee and Ostriker still list what sets the star-formation rate and the initial mass function (IMF) as outstanding questions. They call outflows and disk angular-momentum transport "increasingly well understood" ([McKee & Ostriker 2007](https://www.astro.princeton.edu/~burrows/classes/514/papers/mckee.ostriker.0707.3514.pdf)). The IMF has a lognormal peak at **0.1 to 1 M☉** and a Salpeter-like tail with **α ≈ 2.35**. Its upper cutoff, possibly 100 to 150 M☉, is Debated and may reflect stellar instability rather than formation ([Krumholz 2014](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1402.0867v2.pdf)).

Cooling is the first gate. Primordial gas cools only through H₂ and stalls near **200 K**. Metal-line cooling takes over near **10⁻⁴ to 3×10⁻⁴ Z☉**, and dust-driven fragmentation acts near **10⁻⁶ to 3×10⁻⁵ Z☉**, but only at high density. The cosmic microwave background sets a hard floor of **2.725(1+z) K** ([Bromm & Loeb 2003](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/astro-ph/0310622), [Smith 2009](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0806.1653), [Chiaki 2015](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1410.8384), [Klessen & Glover 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.12500)). The plateau and the CMB floor are Established. The exact shape of the transition is Debated. Molecular gas itself forms mainly on dust grains, and it survives ultraviolet photodissociation through dust absorption and H₂ self-shielding in roughly equal shares ([Krumholz 2014](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1402.0867v2.pdf)). The textbook links that complete this chain are labelled Textbook (unchecked). The Jeans mass scales roughly as T^{3/2} ρ^{−1/2}, so isothermal collapse lowers it as density rises and drives hierarchical fragmentation until the gas turns opaque. Infall rates scale as c_s³/G, so warm primordial gas feeds protostars far faster than 10 K gas does.

Angular momentum is the second gate. Collapse concentrates spin, so matter lands in a disk rather than on the star, and the disk has to shed angular momentum before the star can grow. A disk becomes marginally gravitationally unstable at Toomre **Q ≈ 1**. Saturated gravitational instability gives an effective α of **10⁻² to 1**, with a local limit near 0.1. Fragmentation into companions requires fast cooling, β = t_cool Ω below about 10 to 20 ([Kratter & Lodato 2016](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1603.01280)). Disk fragmentation as a route to binaries is Established. Mature disks behave differently. At 1 AU, non-ideal MHD suppresses MRI turbulence to α ≈ 10⁻⁶, and accretion becomes laminar and wind-driven ([Bai & Stone 2013](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1301.0318)). Whether winds or turbulence drive Class II accretion remains Debated ([Rosotti 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2302.01433)). Present-day Class 0 disks are mostly under **60 AU**, consistent with strong magnetic braking ([Maury 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.11221v2), [Tobin 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.04468)).

Feedback is the third gate, and it acts like a thermostat that caps the most massive stars. In Pop III simulations a bipolar HII region breaks out near 20 M☉, ionizes the gas above the disk, and cuts off the infall that feeds the star. Accretion ended at **43 M☉** in the fiducial run. Disk photoevaporation alone is too slow to stop growth ([Hosokawa et al. 2011](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1111.3649), [McKee & Tan 2008](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0711.4116)). Ionizing output rises from about 10⁴⁵ to 10⁵⁰ photons per second between 5 and 120 M☉ ([Schaerer 2002](https://www.aanda.org/articles/aa/pdf/2002/04/aa1629.pdf)), which is why feedback strengthens so steeply with mass. In enriched gas, disk winds and outflows set a core-to-star efficiency of **0.3 to 0.5** ([Tanaka 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.08856v2)). Newborn stars then disperse their parent clouds, and most stars end up in unbound groups. Why the stars are unbound when the clouds were bound is still open ([Krumholz 2014](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1402.0867v2.pdf)). Triggered star formation (collect-and-collapse, radiation-driven implosion) had no fetched source, so it should not be taught as fact from this reference.

The chain these gates form is the one to teach: **higher metallicity → better cooling → lower temperature → smaller Jeans mass and slower infall → smaller, more numerous stars**, and lower metallicity gives the reverse. That Pop III stars should be massive is Established. How massive is not: simulations span **10 to 1,600 M☉** ([Hirano 2014](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1308.4456)) or about **1 to 300 M☉** ([Susa 2014](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1407.1374)), while stellar archaeology favours under 40 M☉ ([Ishigaki 2018](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1801.07763)).

## A fusion thermostat makes mass the master variable

The main sequence exists because hydrogen fusion is a long-lasting, self-regulating heat source. An overheated core expands, cools and burns more slowly. An underheated core contracts, heats and burns faster. A star of given mass and composition therefore settles on one equilibrium radius and luminosity. This negative-feedback picture is Textbook (unchecked), but it is the most transferable explanation in the subject. Because fusion is so steeply temperature-sensitive, massive stars burn their fuel far faster and live shorter lives despite having more of it: about **1 Myr at 40 M☉, 10 Gyr at 1 M☉ and 200 Gyr at 0.4 M☉** ([OpenStax 22.1](https://openstax.org/books/astronomy/pages/22-1-evolution-from-the-main-sequence-to-red-giants)). The relation flattens at the top end, where radiation pressure dominates. Very massive stars all live about 2 to 3 Myr ([Yusof 2013](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1305.2099)), with a top-end asymptote near **1.9 Myr** ([Köhler 2015](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1501.03794)). Metallicity shifts lifetimes mainly below about 10 M☉: low-Z stars there are hotter and brighter and live up to about 40% shorter at 2 M☉ ([Ekström 2012](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1110.5049), [Georgy 2013](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1308.2914)). Accretion's lifetime and luminosity tables already exist in the earlier stellar-data work. This reference covers only why those tables look the way they do.

There are two hydrogen-burning routes. Stars above about **1.2 M☉** (at Z = 0.02) burn mostly through the CNO cycle, and its much steeper temperature dependence creates convective cores ([Karakas & Lattanzio 2014](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1405.0062)). Below that mass the proton-proton chain dominates. The rest is Textbook (unchecked). The pp chain is throttled by the weak-interaction step p + p → d. CNO uses carbon, nitrogen and oxygen as catalysts, so it needs pre-existing metals. Zero-metal stars must contract hotter until they make trace carbon of their own, which is why Pop III stars are compact and hot.

Three timescales organize everything: the dynamical time (collapse, pulsation), the Kelvin-Helmholtz thermal time (contraction and readjustment) and the nuclear time (the main sequence). Their textbook ordering, t_dyn ≪ t_KH ≪ t_nuc, is Textbook (unchecked). It explains why stars are almost always in equilibrium and why phases that run on the thermal clock, such as pre-main-sequence contraction and the Hertzsprung gap, are short and sparsely populated on the HR diagram. Teach each one as a race between two processes.

## Core mass and degeneracy decide every ending

When core hydrogen runs out, the helium ash core contracts and heats, hydrogen ignites in a shell, and the envelope swells and cools, so the star moves up and to the right on the HR diagram ([OpenStax 22.1](https://openstax.org/books/astronomy/pages/22-1-evolution-from-the-main-sequence-to-red-giants)). What happens next depends on whether the core turns degenerate before it reaches the next ignition temperature. **Core mass, not initial mass, is the deciding variable.** Initial mass maps onto core mass only through mass loss, mixing and binarity, and those are the uncertain links.

Low-mass stars ignite helium under degenerate conditions in a "violent and explosive" flash. The upper mass for this is about **2.1 M☉** at Z = 0.02 without overshoot and about **1.75 M☉** at [Fe/H] = −2.3 ([Karakas & Lattanzio 2014](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1405.0062)). Along the way, convective mixing brings burning products to the surface. The first dredge-up on the red giant branch raises surface helium by about 0.03 and lowers ¹²C/¹³C from about 89 to 18–26. Above about 4 M☉ a second dredge-up raises surface helium by up to 0.1. On the AGB, helium-shell thermal pulses followed by third dredge-up bring carbon and s-process elements to the surface and can make carbon stars. In intermediate-mass AGB stars, hot-bottom burning adds proton-capture products. Core helium burning lasts about **100 Myr** for low-mass stars and about **13 Myr at 6 M☉**. AGB models still rest on "highly uncertain assumptions about the treatment of convection and mass loss" (Debated) ([Karakas & Lattanzio 2014](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1405.0062)). Super-AGB stars ignite carbon off-centre and usually leave oxygen-neon white dwarfs. The electron-capture supernova window is narrow, perhaps **0.1 to 0.2 M☉** ([Doherty 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1410.5431)). Planetary-nebula physics was not sourced.

Massive stars burn through carbon, neon, oxygen and silicon in successively shorter stages inside an onion-skin structure, cooled by neutrinos, until an iron core forms that fusion cannot power. The collapse sequence is Established. Collapse begins at γ ≤ 4/3 and takes **150 to 350 ms**. Electron captures neutronize the core and neutrinos become trapped. The core bounces at nuclear density. Within about **5 ms** the shock stalls at **100 to 200 km**, weakened by the neutrino burst and by photodissociation. Neutrino heating in the "gain region" behind the shock, helped by convection, must then overcome accretion ram pressure of **0.05 to 1 M☉/s**. Only a canonical **10⁵¹ erg** of the roughly **3×10⁵³ erg** binding energy becomes the explosion ([Burrows 2013](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1210.4921)). The delayed neutrino mechanism is "emerging as a robust solution," but it is not yet fully confirmed ([Burrows & Vartanyan 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.14157v3)). The magnetorotational alternative needs millisecond core spin, and pulsar spins suggest it is rare, at most about 1% of collapses ([Burrows 2013](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1210.4921)).

Whether a star explodes, called explodability, is the most game-relevant open problem. "More massive stars are more difficult to explode," and the "progenitor is destiny" through the core's density profile ([Burrows 2013](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1210.4921)). But the dependence on mass is not monotonic. Failure islands sit at **22 to 25 and 27 to 30 M☉** ([Sukhbold 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1510.04643)). 3D models find black holes after strong explosions and failures at 12 to 14 M☉ ([Burrows 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.07831)). Observed type II-P progenitors top out near **18 to 19 M☉**, the "red supergiant problem" ([Smartt 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.02635)). At the extreme, helium cores of **40 to 63 M☉** pulsate and shed mass but still collapse. Cores of **64 to 133 M☉** (about 140 to 260 M☉ initial) are disrupted entirely by pair instability, leave no remnant and make up to **57 M☉ of ⁵⁶Ni**. Above that range, black holes form directly ([Heger & Woosley 2002](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0107037)). The result is a predicted black-hole mass gap of about **52 to 133 M☉** ([Woosley 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.08939)), whose lower edge sits near 43 to 46 M☉ in [Farmer 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.12874). Both edges depend on the ¹²C(α,γ) rate (Debated) ([Takahashi 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.05373)).

## Most massive stars live in pairs, which rewrites their endings

Binary interaction is the main evolutionary channel for massive stars, not a footnote: "more than 70% of all massive stars will exchange mass with a companion," and a merger results "in one-third of the cases" ([Sana et al. 2012](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012Sci...337..444S/abstract)). The geometry is Textbook (unchecked). A star that swells past its Roche lobe, the equipotential teardrop around it, hands mass to its companion. Transfer from the heavier star shrinks the orbit and tends to run away. Transfer from the lighter star widens the orbit and tends to stay stable. Stable transfer strips the donor and spins up and rejuvenates the gainer. Runaway transfer leads to a common envelope. Inside a common envelope, friction drains orbital energy into the gas. The outcome is either ejection, which leaves a tight binary, or a merger, and "when each outcome occurs is not understood from first principles." Ivanova and colleagues call it "arguably the most significant and least-well-constrained major process in binary evolution" (Debated). Its products include SN Ia progenitors, X-ray binaries, double neutron stars and gravitational-wave sources ([Ivanova et al. 2013](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1209.4302)).

Type Ia supernovae show how a binary turns a stable remnant into an explosion. A C-O white dwarf in a close binary ignites carbon near its centre, deflagrates and then detonates, releasing about 10⁵¹ erg. Its light comes from the decay **⁵⁶Ni → ⁵⁶Co → ⁵⁶Fe** ([SN Ia progenitor review, arXiv:1204.1155](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1204.1155)). The explosion physics is Established. The progenitor is Debated. In the single-degenerate channel the white dwarf accretes from a normal star, but only within a narrow window of accretion rate. Too fast leads to a common envelope. Too slow gives novae that eject the accreted mass. In the double-degenerate channel two white dwarfs spiral together through gravitational-wave emission and merge, at the risk of collapsing to a neutron star instead. A sub-Chandrasekhar double detonation triggered by a helium shell is a third option. The Ia rate is the star-formation history convolved with a delay-time distribution, and whether that distribution is bimodal or follows t⁻¹ is disputed (same review). Binary evolution is also not expected to fill the pair-instability black-hole gap ([Woosley 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.08939)). Blue stragglers, nova runaways and the classes of X-ray binary are standard results, but no source was fetched for them, so they should be treated as Textbook (unchecked).

## Stars seed the next generation on staggered clocks

Stars return mass, energy and new nuclei to the gas that forms the next stars, and each source has its own delay. That delay structure is what makes chemical enrichment teachable as a sequence of unlocks.

| Source | Typical delay after formation | Main contribution | Label |
|---|---|---|---|
| Big Bang | Before stars | H, He, trace Li | Textbook (unchecked) |
| Core-collapse supernovae | About 3 to 40 Myr | α-elements, some iron | Established |
| Failed supernovae (direct collapse) | Same | Wind material only, so sharply reduced metals ([Limongi & Chieffi 2018 via NuPyCEE](https://github.com/NuGrid/NuPyCEE/blob/master/yield_tables/agb_and_massive_stars_K10_LC18_R000.txt)) | Simplified |
| AGB winds | 0.1 to many Gyr | C, N, s-process (Ba, Pb peaks), first-peak Sr/Y/Zr with electron-capture SNe ([Kobayashi, Karakas & Lugaro 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.04660v1)) | Established |
| Type Ia supernovae | About 0.1 to 10 Gyr | Most of the iron peak ([arXiv:1204.1155](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1204.1155)) | Established; delay shape Debated |
| Neutron-star mergers | Long, merger-limited | r-process to Th and U, confirmed by the GW170817 kilonova ([Siegel 2019](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1901.09044)) | Established that they produce it; share Debated |
| Magnetorotational SNe or collapsars | Short | Possibly much early r-process (about 3% of 25 to 50 M☉ hypernovae in Kobayashi 2020; collapsars in Siegel 2019) | Debated |
| Pair-instability SNe | About 2 to 3 Myr | Roughly solar even-Z pattern, odd-Z deficit, nothing above Zn ([Heger & Woosley 2002](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0107037)) | Established within models |

Two contrasts make this table teachable. The s-process captures neutrons more slowly than nuclei β-decay, so it walks along the valley of stability. The r-process needs neutron fluxes high enough that captures outpace decays, and lanthanides additionally require Ye below about 0.25 ([Siegel 2019](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1901.09044)). GW170817's kilonova showed both a blue, lanthanide-poor component of about 0.016 M☉ and a red, lanthanide-rich component of about 0.05 M☉. Neutron-star mergers, however, are "too slow to explain observations at low metallicities" ([Kobayashi et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.04660v1)). Hot outflows from ordinary supernovae, once the favoured r-process site, are now disfavoured ([Siegel 2019](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1901.09044)). Enrichment is also local and patchy. Cosmic mean metallicity reaches about **10⁻³ Z☉ by z ≈ 6** ([Madau & Dickinson 2014](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1403.0007)), but only about **10⁻⁴ of cosmic volume** exceeded 10⁻⁴ Z☉ at z = 7.5 ([Jaacks 2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.07372)). This closes the loop back to cooling. Each generation's yields lower the next generation's Jeans mass, which drives the switch from Pop III to Pop II. The [α/Fe] "knee" produced by the delay between core-collapse and Type Ia supernovae is the standard chemical clock. It follows from the delay-time distribution, but no fetched source stated it directly.

## The HR diagram is a population map, not a path

The HR diagram plots luminosity against surface temperature, with hot stars on the left. A star's position encodes its radius through L = 4πR²σT⁴ (Textbook (unchecked)). The main sequence is where stars burn core hydrogen. Giants and supergiants sit upper right because they are large and cool, and white dwarfs sit lower left because they are small and hot. Stars leave the main sequence by moving up and to the right. More massive stars pass through each stage faster, for example about **1.2×10⁷ yr** to the end of the 15 M☉ track ([OpenStax 22.1](https://openstax.org/books/astronomy/pages/22-1-evolution-from-the-main-sequence-to-red-giants)). At low metallicity, zero-age stars below about 3 M☉ sit hotter and 0.25 to 0.4 dex brighter at fixed mass (MIST, via the earlier stellar-data report). How crowded each region looks is set by how long stars stay there. That is why the main sequence is dense, the Hertzsprung gap is sparse, and a cluster's turnoff works as an age clock. Hayashi and Henyey tracks, blue loops and white-dwarf cooling tracks are standard, but none were sourced this session.

# Part II — Teaching guidance: how a game should explain this physics

## Players arrive believing stars burn and that size matters more than mass

The largest pre-instruction study of star ideas surveyed **2,276 non-science majors** at one US university. It found that **44%** describe stars as "burning." **32%** say stars make light by burning and **28%** by chemical reactions, while only **7%** name fusion. **48%** say star-forming material "comes together" by an unspecified mechanism, and only **about 16%** name gravity. When asked how stars differ, **68%** cite size, while mass appears in only about **5.5%** of responses. Colour is mentioned by 19% and never linked to temperature ([Bailey et al. 2009](https://istardb.org/wp-content/uploads/tainacan-items/1018/18766/Bailey-et-al_2009_College-Students-Preinstructional-Ideas-About-Stars-and-Star-Formation-1-1.pdf)). The authors blame everyday meanings of "burning," "dying star" and "energy," and they recommend contrasting fusion with combustion explicitly. The evidence is Strong for one institution. The Star Properties Concept Inventory, validated with more than 2,000 students at two institutions, is built on these ideas ([PhysPort SPCI](https://www.physport.org/assessments/SPCI)). Light is similarly opaque to novices: national pretest scores on the Light and Spectroscopy Concept Inventory cluster around **25%**, near chance ([Prather et al. 2009](https://physicstoday.aip.org/features/teaching-and-learning-astronomy-in-the-21st-century), [Rudolph et al. 2010](https://home.miracosta.edu/rfrench/documents/rudolph_etal_aer10.pdf)).

| Belief players bring | Prevalence | Evidence | What the game must make visible |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stars burn like fire, or run on chemistry | 44% "burning"; 7% name fusion | Strong (n = 2,276, one site) | Fusion changes one element into another; no oxygen, no flame |
| Clouds "come together" without gravity | 16% name gravity | Strong (same study) | Gravity as the visible cause of collapse |
| Size, not mass, is what distinguishes stars | Mass in about 5.5% of answers | Strong (same study) | Mass as the input; size and colour as consequences |
| Red means hot | About 80% of novices vs 60% of experts | Moderate (more than 8,000 online respondents) ([Smith, Arcand et al. 2010](https://www.capjournal.org/issues/10/10_13.pdf)) | Blue = hottest, stated wherever colour appears |
| Planets are bigger than stars | Nearly half of grade 8–10 pupils; instruction did not fix it | Moderate (one country) ([Rajpaul et al. 2018](https://physics.aps.org/articles/v11/93)) | Zoomable, relational scale |
| A shooting star is a dying star | 16% | Strong (Bailey 2009) | Pair "dying" metaphors with the physical event |
| Black holes are cosmic vacuum cleaners; black holes last forever | Common; no sample reported for the first | Weak ([Eisenhamer 2004](https://aasarchives.blob.core.windows.net/archives/BAAS/v36n2/aas204/99.htm)); Moderate for persistence ([Favia et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1206.2302)) | Same pull as any equal mass at equal distance; horizon as the point of no return |
| More massive stars live longer; the main sequence is a path stars travel | Not measured | Design inference | Encounters where the wrong prediction visibly fails |

Two limits matter for a game designer. No rigorous study of novice ideas about stellar death (red giants, supernovae, neutron stars) was found, and Bailey et al. flag that gap themselves. And knowing some astronomy is no protection. In a regression on 910 students, prior math and physical-science courses predicted gains, but a prior astronomy course did not ([Rudolph et al. 2010](https://home.miracosta.edu/rfrench/documents/rudolph_etal_aer10.pdf)). Teaching order is also less important than it seems. Teaching the least-persistent misconceptions first produced identical gains, so the authors recommend spending more time on the most persistent ones instead ([Favia et al., Physics Today](https://physicstoday.aip.org/quick-study/taking-on-astronomy-misconceptions-isnt-easy)).

## Guided interaction roughly doubles gains, and unguided discovery loses

The most consistent result across astronomy, physics and simulation research is that learners must act on a concept within a structure that gives feedback. A national study of nearly **4,000 students in 69 Astro 101 sections at 31 institutions** found that sections with low interactivity averaged a normalized gain of **0.13**, while high-interactivity sections averaged **0.29** (p < 10⁻⁵). No low-interactivity class exceeded 0.30 ([Prather et al. 2009](https://physicstoday.aip.org/features/teaching-and-learning-astronomy-in-the-21st-century)). Interactivity still explained only about a quarter of the variance, so how well a technique is implemented matters as much as which one is chosen. The study's interactivity score also measured time spent, not quality ([Rudolph et al. 2010](https://home.miracosta.edu/rfrench/documents/rudolph_etal_aer10.pdf)).

| Technique or comparison | Effect | Evidence | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Interactive engagement vs lecture (physics) | ⟨g⟩ ≈ 0.48 vs 0.23 over 62 courses | Strong (figures not re-checked) | [Hake 1998](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.18809) |
| Interactivity in Astro 101 | 0.29 vs 0.13 gain; all subgroups benefit | Strong | [Prather 2009](https://physicstoday.aip.org/features/teaching-and-learning-astronomy-in-the-21st-century) |

The meta-analyses agree on what makes games and simulations work. Added scaffolding or feedback helps most. Multiple sessions beat one, and the number of sessions moderated achievement in D'Angelo. Play works better as a supplement to instruction or debriefing than on its own (Wouters, Sitzmann). Two warnings follow. First, "games are motivating" is not a sound justification, because Wouters found no reliable motivational advantage. Second, letting players "generate the rule" with no feedback scored worse than the comparison conditions. Productive failure works only when a consolidation phase follows the struggle: comparing the attempt with the canonical solution and explaining why the attempt failed ([Sinha & Kapur 2021](https://ouci.dntb.gov.ua/works/4KnMgZq7); consolidation design per [Kapur 2016](https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2016.1155457), not re-checked). Inquiry learners have well-documented weaknesses. They change several variables at once, design experiments that can only confirm, and misread graphs. Model progression, hypothesis scratchpads and structured prompts help ([de Jong & van Joolingen 1998](https://research.utwente.nl/en/publications/scientific-discovery-learning-with-computer-simulations-of-concep/), not re-checked). This matters for idle games in particular, which encourage buying several upgrades at once. That defeats control of variables unless the game offers a one-variable comparison (Design inference).

These classroom formats translate into specific solo-game patterns, all Design inference. **Predict–observe–explain**: the player commits to a forecast ("what will this 25 M☉ core become?") before the simulation resolves it, and the game then gives the reason. This draws on elicited explanation (d = 0.36) and the commit-before-reveal step of Peer Instruction. **Ranking tasks** become "order these stars by lifetime" or "by core temperature" puzzles. **Lecture-Tutorial sequencing** becomes a short chain of targeted questions that walks a player through one misconception, such as why the heavier star dies first. In each case the player must do cognitive work, not just click. Mayer's review of three research lines concludes that cognitive activity, not behavioural activity, drives learning ([Mayer 2004](https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.59.1.14), not re-checked).

## Make the physics the mechanic, then relax one constraint per layer

Intrinsic integration has the clearest game-specific support. In *Zombie Division*, children learned more when division was the combat mechanic than when the same maths appeared as quizzes between levels, and they chose to play the intrinsic version much longer. This is a Moderate result from a single study, with figures not re-checked ([Habgood & Ainsworth 2011](https://doi.org/10.1080/10508406.2010.508029)). Foldit points the same way. Its score was a real energy model, and players' protein solutions rivalled the Rosetta algorithm on some targets ([Cooper et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature09304), not re-checked). For Accretion, this means upgrades should change physical parameters, such as cooling, turbulence α, metallicity and accretion rate, and the simulated physics should decide the outcome. An upgrade that directly buys "more fusion" would teach the procedural-rhetoric lesson that SimCity's critics warned about: a game's embedded rules teach their assumptions as if they were neutral facts ([Starr 1994](https://swh.princeton.edu/~starr/17star.html), [Bogost 2007](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262514880/persuasive-games/)). Kerbal Space Program's reputation for teaching counterintuitive orbital facts rests on a simplified but genuine law (patched conics) and cheap, visible failure ([explain xkcd 1356](https://www.explainxkcd.com/wiki/index.php/1356:_Orbital_Mechanics)). That is anecdotal evidence, Weak, but consistent with the controlled findings.

PhET's design framework gives the interface rules. Its core idea is "implicit scaffolding guides without students feeling guided," through affordances, constraints, cueing and feedback: "correct usage is guaranteed by design" ([Podolefsky, Moore & Perkins 2013](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1306.6544)). In Energy Skate Park, each tab relaxes the previous tab's constraint: the Intro tab fixes friction at zero, the next adds friction, and a Playground tab allows a limited free build. Other PhET rules include a first interaction that needs no text, minimal wording, a prominent reset, linked representations that change in sync, immediate feedback, bounded parameter ranges that show illuminating extreme cases, and visible renderings of invisible quantities. The direct evidence for implicit scaffolding is Weak: one think-aloud, which the authors call "not generalizable." The meta-analytic support for scaffolded simulations (g = 0.49) is Strong. The think-aloud also carries a warning. The student's belief that heavier skaters go faster survived until she tested it directly with the speedometer, so a simulation that merely fails to contradict a misconception will not remove it. The design has to make the wrong intuition fail in front of the player.

The Accretion mapping is direct (Design inference). Each prestige layer relaxes one physical constraint, in the same way that PhET tabs, Nicky Case's "teach each mechanic in isolation, then combine" ([Case](https://blog.ncase.me/explorable-explanations)) and de Jong's model progression all recommend. A suggested order is fixed metallicity first, then metallicity-dependent cooling, then explodability islands, then binaries, then enrichment clocks. Presets such as a 0.1 M☉ and a 50 M☉ star serve as illuminating extremes. Live gauges for core temperature, the pressure-gravity balance and the fusion rate apply "show the invisible." The earlier stellar-data work recommended implementing explosion islands as a deterministic function of mass rather than a random draw. That choice is also the right teaching choice, because a deterministic map is learnable and a random draw is not. Bret Victor's "ladder of abstraction" gives the visual progression ([Victor](https://worrydream.com/LadderOfAbstraction/), not re-checked): one star's live track on the HR diagram, then lifetime against mass across every star the player has formed, then a population HR diagram with a turnoff. Unlocking higher rungs fits naturally as prestige rewards. Gating explanations until the player has tried a task raised motivation to seek them in *Earth: A Primer* playtests ([Case](https://blog.ncase.me/explorable-explanations)). That is a Weak, practitioner observation.

Cognitive load sets the pacing. Techniques that help novices, such as worked examples and integrated text plus diagrams, lose value or backfire as expertise grows. This is the expertise-reversal effect, Strong but not re-checked ([Kalyuga et al. 2003](https://doi.org/10.1207/S15326985EP3801_4)). The game should therefore fade tooltips after correct predictions and keep the equations in an optional layer, not in the main flow. Clark and colleagues report that schematic visuals did as well as or better than cartoon or realistic ones (not re-checked), so a clean live HR diagram is a better teaching investment than photoreal nebulae.

## Write every explanation as a refutation, a causal chain and a layered label

Text in the game should do three jobs: displace the wrong model, tell a causal story, and stay short. Refutation format is the best-supported way to do the first (Strong): state the common belief, say plainly that it is wrong, and give the scientific account. Conceptual-change theory explains why. A learner drops an idea only when they are dissatisfied with it and the replacement is intelligible, plausible and fruitful ([Posner et al. 1982](https://doi.org/10.1002/sce.3730660207), not re-checked). For narrative, the evidence is Moderate. Text built on causality, temporality and character is read about **twice as fast** and recalled about **twice as well** as expository text. Narratives are also "intrinsically persuasive" and hard to correct, so fine details may relax but "core scientific processes" must stay accurate ([Dahlstrom 2014](https://uvm.edu/~mjk/195%20Winter%20Tracking%20Specialty/Using%20narratives%20and%20storytelling%20to%20communicate%20science%20with%20nonexpert%20audiences.pdf)). Randy Olson's And-But-Therefore template ([SESYNC](https://www.sesync.org/resources/communications-toolkit-and-therefore-statement)) is a practitioner device (Weak) that compresses the causal arc into one tooltip. A worked example is "The core runs out of hydrogen, BUT gravity never stops, THEREFORE the core shrinks and heats until helium ignites." Anthropomorphic framing, such as a cloud that "wants" to collapse, is acceptable under Dahlstrom's ethics as long as the mechanism stays right.

Analogies need explicit limits. Glynn's Teaching-With-Analogies model ends with "indicate where the analogy breaks down" ([Glynn](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/242536011_Making_Science_Concepts_Meaningful_to_Students_Teaching_with_Analogies), framework, not re-checked). The only peer-reviewed analogy critique found shows that a marble on warped spandex follows T³ ∝ r² or T ∝ r, not Kepler's T² ∝ r³, because the sheet's own material governs the motion ([Middleton & Langston via Physics arXiv Blog](https://medium.com/the-physics-arxiv-blog/relativity-shock-experiments-reveal-that-deformed-rubber-sheet-is-not-like-spacetime-b8566ba5a110)). Ted Bunn argues that the rubber-sheet picture of expansion gives "precisely the wrong intuitions" about relativity ([Bunn](https://blog.richmond.edu/physicsbunn/?p=66)). For the stellar analogies themselves, no evaluation studies exist. The table below gives the researchers' physics assessments, which are Design inference on the teaching side.

| Concept | Common analogy | Where it breaks | Better framing for the game |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fusion | "Burning," a nuclear furnace | No oxygen, no flame; reinforces the top misconception | Squeezing nuclei until they stick; a little mass becomes energy |
| Hydrostatic balance | Balloon, tug-of-war | A star has no skin; the balance is local at every layer | A thermostat: squeeze → hotter → faster fusion → re-expand |
| Degeneracy | Crowded room, no two in one seat | Not a repulsion; works at zero temperature | A "But" twist: adding mass makes a white dwarf smaller |
| Jeans collapse | Cloud too heavy to hold itself up | Turbulence, fields, rotation and cooling also matter; clouds fragment | A race between free-fall and the pressure response |
| Angular momentum | Spinning skater | Implies collapse proceeds unimpeded | Spin traps gas in a disk, which must shed spin before the star grows |
| Accretion disk | Water down a drain | Disks need turbulence or winds to move spin outward | Gas spirals in only as fast as it hands spin outward |
| Supernova | A bomb | Core collapse is an implosion and rebound; Ia is a different mechanism | Show core collapse and Ia as separate events |
| Black hole | Vacuum cleaner | Pulls no harder than an equal-mass star at equal distance | The point of no return |

Interacting processes are easiest to explain as named structures (Design inference, built on Dahlstrom's causality finding). A **negative feedback loop** stabilizes: the main-sequence thermostat, or feedback capping stellar mass. A **positive feedback loop** runs away: the helium flash, white-dwarf carbon ignition, or unstable mass transfer. A small diagram with "+" and "−" arrows conveys the sign at a glance. A **race between timescales** decides the outcome, such as cooling time against free-fall time, or infall rate against feedback. A **balance point** is where two arrows meet, and the explanation should say what happens if the system is nudged either way.

Length and layering follow museum and astronomy-image research. Serrell's label guidance is **60 words or fewer** at a 12th-grade level or below, drawn from research showing that more visitors read shorter labels ([AAM review of Serrell](https://www.aam-us.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/19_Exhibition_BookReview_ExhibitLabels.pdf); Weak to Moderate, as only the review was read). The Aesthetics & Astronomy study found that context is "critical to comprehension, particularly for novices," and that captions even raised novices' enjoyment. Novices prefer narrative text and experts short technical text, and the study recommends rollovers of the form "here is what astronomers see" ([Smith, Arcand et al. 2010](https://www.capjournal.org/issues/10/10_13.pdf); Moderate). That supports a four-layer system (Design inference). **L0** is a 5 to 12 word name and hook. **L1** is an ABT or refutation tooltip of 25 to 50 words. **L2** is an expandable "how it works / what astronomers see" panel of 60 to 150 words with a colour key or scale bar. **L3** is a codex entry with numbers, ranges, the governing equation and an "Open question" note. Expertise reversal argues for keeping L2 and L3 optional.

## Encode colour, scale and the HR diagram with keys and staged axes

Visual encodings carry their own misconceptions, and professionals handle them with transparency. Astronomy image makers recommend the term **"representative colour"** over "false colour," because a mapped image "is not wrong or phony." They map energy to hue in "chromatic order" (low energy red, high energy blue), publish a colour legend "as weather maps do," and warn that believing images are "faked" erodes trust ([Rector, Arcand et al., arXiv:1308.5237](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1308.5237)). Scale bars and colour keys help comprehension at every expertise level without overwhelming viewers ([Smith, Arcand et al. 2010](https://www.capjournal.org/issues/10/10_13.pdf)). Because about 80% of novices read red as hot, every star-colour display should carry a one-line "blue = hottest" cue. An "eye vs telescope" toggle between visible light and X-ray, infrared or H-alpha, which applies A&A's multiwavelength recommendation, teaches that colour is data (Design inference).

The HR diagram needs staging. An eye-tracking study found it imposes significantly higher cognitive load than ordinary x–y graphs. The load comes from the reversed temperature axis, logarithmic luminosity and spectral labels, and students misapplied x–y graph habits to it (Moderate to Weak, n = 35) ([PRPER 2022](https://publications.goettingen-research-online.de/ID-116709)). So introduce the axes one at a time, colour↔temperature first and then luminosity, and label the reversed axis explicitly. Show evolution as a moving dot, contrasted with the main sequence drawn as a population, to block the "line = path over time" misreading (Design inference). Scale resists ordinary instruction. Students who could say in one sentence what two objects are almost always ranked their sizes correctly, and the researchers recommend zoomable visualizations ([Rajpaul et al. 2018](https://physics.aps.org/articles/v11/93); Moderate). Dahlstrom adds that "the farther the perception is from human scale, the less accurate it is likely to be." An incremental game's exponential numbers suit a labelled powers-of-ten zoom and relational anchors better than raw values. A logarithmic UI axis must be taught, not assumed (Design inference; no controlled study of log-scale instruction in astronomy was found).

## Label the model honestly: physics rule, game rule, open question

No study has tested in-game "real versus game rule" labelling. Five lines of evidence nonetheless converge on it. PhET simplifies deliberately and then relaxes the simplifications. Victor argues that the models behind claims should be exposed so readers can test them ([Explorable Explanations](https://worrydream.com/ExplorableExplanations/)). The procedural-rhetoric critiques show that hidden models teach their assumptions. The meta-analyses find that play works better supplemented by instruction. And the GMAT orbit study's authors caution that simulation should complement analytical teaching, not replace it ([Berthoud & Walsh 2020](https://core.ac.uk/download/322476793.pdf)). That study is also the closest template for an astronomy game. It targeted named misconceptions with one-parameter-at-a-time exercises and raised scores from **68% to 83%** (n = 37 matched, no control group), and students found the unguided final exercise hard (Weak to Moderate).

The evidence on uncertainty is direct. Across five experiments with about **5,780** UK participants, giving a numeric range slightly lowered trust in the number but **did not** lower trust in the source. Vague verbal hedges did more damage ([van der Bles et al. 2020 summary](https://blog.aiimpacts.org/p/paper-summary-the-effects-of-communicating); Moderate to Strong). Debated physics should therefore appear as ranges with an "Open question" tag, for example "failure islands at 22–25 and 27–30 M☉; which stars explode is still debated." It should not appear as "scientists aren't sure." Accretion's existing `FIDELITY.md` labels (Established, Simplified, Debated) can be shown to players directly. Debated items are the natural places for in-game discovery, because a player's surprise there matches a real open question.

Four concrete honesty mechanics follow (Design inference). First, give every physics-driven stat a consistent icon and a "model card" naming its equation and validity range, and give currency, multipliers and offline acceleration a different icon. Second, disclose time compression beside the game clock, for example "this collapse took 10 Myr; you watched 7 minutes." No study shows that compression causes timescale misconceptions, but it is the plausible risk. Third, label necessary pacing interventions as rules. The earlier stellar-data report found that a realistic default star of 17 to 22 M☉ lands where explosions are unreliable, so a guaranteed first supernova should say it is a tutorial rule. Fourth, turn the prestige screen into a debrief. It should summarize what the run demonstrated and ask the player to predict the next run, which pairs the consolidation phase of productive failure with the meta-analytic "supplement" moderator.

## Conclusion

The physics and the pedagogy point toward the same architecture. The causal loop of metallicity, cooling, mass, core, ending, yield and metallicity again maps onto Accretion's prestige cycle. And the strongest teaching evidence (scaffolded simulation, guided discovery, prediction followed by consolidation, multiple sessions with a debrief) describes what an idle game already does well, provided the reset is used for reflection rather than only for multipliers. The less obvious finding is where the risk lies. It is not in oversimplified physics, which PhET-style progressive simplification handles well. It is in the economy layer, where exponential costs, bundled upgrades and invisible time compression can teach false causality more persuasively than any tooltip can correct. Separating physics rules from game rules is therefore a core teaching mechanic, not cosmetic honesty.

For the reusable skill, the two parts should become separate reference files. One is a physics file keyed by mechanism, with Established, Simplified, Debated and Textbook (unchecked) labels. The other is a teaching file keyed by technique, with Strong, Moderate, Weak and Design inference labels. The bridging map acts as the index that joins them. Three gaps should be closed before the skill hard-codes them. The textbook anchors (virial theorem, Chandrasekhar mass, pp/CNO temperature exponents, Roche-lobe stability) need a fetched textbook citation. Clark 2016, Habgood and Ainsworth, and Hake need their figures re-checked. And no study yet tests any of these techniques inside an idle or incremental game, so Accretion's own playtests, run with SPCI-style pre/post items on lifetime-versus-mass and fusion-versus-burning, would be the first evidence of their kind.
