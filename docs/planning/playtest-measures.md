# Playtest measures

> Planning note; last updated 2026-11-08. Playtest questionnaire design, untested until a session runs.

No study has tested these teaching methods inside an idle game, so Accretion's playtests can supply the first evidence. Ask the same short questions before a player's first session and after several sessions, and compare.

## Format

- Five to eight multiple-choice questions, under five minutes.
- Ask before play and after a set amount of play, such as three sessions.
- Phrase items around concepts, not game terms, so they measure physics understanding rather than recall of the interface.
- The Star Properties Concept Inventory is a validated instrument worth borrowing from where its license allows.

## Candidate questions

| Concept | Example question | Misconception it targets |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What powers a star | What makes a star give off light? | Stars burn like fire |
| What forms a star | What makes a gas cloud become a star? | Clouds come together without a cause |
| Mass and lifetime | Which lives longer, a heavier star or a lighter one? | Bigger stars live longer |
| What distinguishes stars | What property most decides how a star will live and die? | Size matters more than mass |
| Star color | Which star is hottest: red, yellow, or blue? | Red means hot |
| Where planet materials come from | Where did the iron in Earth come from? | The Big Bang made the solar system |
| Lagrange points | Is an object at L4 held in place because gravity cancels there? | Gravity cancels |
| Novae | Does a nova usually make a white dwarf heavier? | The white dwarf fills up until it explodes |

## Also measure

- **Prediction accuracy over time:** Share of correct predictions per relationship, from the prediction system.
- **Help use:** How often players ask for hints, and at which tiers.
- **Confounded runs:** Share of runs that change several settings, as a sign of whether control of variables is being learned.
- **Observation:** Watch players without explaining anything, and note where they stall or misread a control.
